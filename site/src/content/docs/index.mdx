FPLinux is an open-source Linux system for keypad phones built on the Unisoc
UMS9117 chip. It drives the phone's screen and keypad and, depending on the
phone, its sound, FM radio, Bluetooth and microSD card, and it gives you a real
shell on the phone and from your computer.

## How it works

FPLinux runs from the phone's RAM. To keep files and installed packages between
sessions on a supported phone, boot from a
[microSD system card](/fplinux/guides/microsd-system-card/).
FPLinux cannot be installed to the phone's internal storage.

## What you get

The [feature overview](/fplinux/features/) separates implementation from
device checks. Your phone's checklist records its supported functions and limits.

## What you need

- One of the phones above and a USB data cable.
- A Linux x86-64 PC with Python 3.14 and network access for the first build.
