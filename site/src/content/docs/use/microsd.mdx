In the normal RAM boot, everything you create on the phone disappears at power-off. A microSD card keeps files between sessions. This page covers a card used for data, formatted as FAT32 or ext4.

Check [microSD support](/fplinux/phones/#support-status) for your phone and use only the profiles and filesystems listed on its [phone page](/fplinux/phones/). A card slot does not by itself mean that FPLinux can use the card.

<FeatureStatus feature="removable-storage" />

To run the whole system from a card instead, see [microSD system card](/fplinux/guides/microsd-system-card/). A system card must stay installed and mounted while Linux runs; the steps on this page do not apply to it.

## Insert the card

Follow the slot and insertion instructions on your [phone page](/fplinux/phones/). If the slot is under the battery, insert the card before starting the phone. Insert or remove a card while Linux runs only when the phone page permits it, and only while it is unmounted.

After inserting a supported data card, open [Files](/fplinux/use/files/) and choose **Options → Refresh**. The places list shows **Memory card** when it is detected. Detection has no fixed delay.

## Mount the card

Select **Memory card** in Files to connect it, then select it again to browse. Files uses the first partition, or the whole card when it has no partition table. It accepts FAT32 and ext4 on phones that support them. An unsupported format is reported without formatting or changing the card.

The connected card is available at `/mnt/card` for [file transfers](/fplinux/use/connect/) and shell commands. FAT32 is connected with access for the application user. An ext4 card keeps its existing ownership and permissions, so some directories may be readable but not writable by applications. A read-only card is marked as such.

A system card, a card with active swap or a card mounted elsewhere cannot be disconnected through Files. Internal eMMC is not selected as a data card.

## Remove the card safely

:::caution

Never remove a mounted card or a card with active swap, and never remove a card during a write. The filesystem on the card can be damaged.

:::

<Steps>

1. Close applications and shell sessions that use files on the card.

2. If you enabled swap on the data card, turn it off with `swapoff`. Never remove a card that supplies the running system root.

3. Return to **Places** in Files, select **Memory card**, then choose **Options → Safely eject**. Wait until its description changes to **Select to connect**. If it is busy, close the application using it and try again. Files does not force an unmount.

4. Remove the card only as allowed on your phone page. If accessing the slot requires removing the battery, [end the session safely](/fplinux/use/power/) first.

</Steps>

For a phone with supported hot swap, refresh Places after removing the disconnected card before inserting another one. Without hot-swap support, leave the card installed until shutdown, even after disconnecting it in Files; start a new session after changing it.

## Limitations

- Internal storage cannot hold your files through FPLinux. Supported UMS9117 phones provide [read-only raw NAND backup](/fplinux/guides/device-data/#save-a-nand-backup); Qin reads selected sound files from its read-only eMMC. Keep files on a supported card or copy them to your computer.
- Use hot swap only when the exact phone's checklist permits it. Otherwise insert the card before boot and leave it installed until shutdown, including when powered-on replacement is untested. Check the phone page for the supported profiles and filesystems.
- Stock-firmware hot swap, a physically accessible slot and FPLinux hot swap are separate facts. Check the phone's **microSD hot swap** result; SIM hot swap is recorded separately and is not supported in FPLinux. Never remove a system card while Linux uses it as its root, even on a phone that supports data-card hot swap.
- Card detection has no fixed delay; wait for the device to appear or, on a hot-swap target, disappear.
