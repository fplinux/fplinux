FPLinux: ARMADA is a showcase for the phone. It renders a 3D scene on the phone
itself, while the LCD brightness, the keypad light and the vibrator follow the
same timeline. One cycle of the presentation lasts 38 seconds; it repeats until
you exit.

<FeatureStatus feature="fplinux-armada" />

Check [phone support status](/fplinux/phones/#support-status) for the physical
effects available on your phone.

## Install and start

Install `fplinux-showcase.apk` as described in
[Packages](/fplinux/guides/packages/). In the default RAM profile, it lasts
until shutdown; on a microSD system card, it stays installed until you remove
it. Follow the [system-card shutdown rules](/fplinux/use/power/) before cutting
power. Start the application in the local terminal or from the PC, as described
in [Start an application](/fplinux/apps/):

```sh title="Phone"
fplinux-showcase
```

To stop on its own after a number of complete cycles, pass `--runs`:

```sh title="Phone"
fplinux-showcase --runs 1
```

The application needs a `240×320` or `128×160` display, the right soft key, the
system brightness service, the keypad light and the vibrator. If one of them is
missing, it does not start and names the missing part.

## What you see and feel

Daylight and LCD brightness change throughout the presentation. The keypad
light and the vibrator switch on and off in time with the scene; their pulse
timing changes, not their intensity. During the hardware act, the keypad light
and the vibrator spell `FPLINUX` in Morse code while the matching letter pulses
on the screen. The cycle ends with two seconds of water after the letters sink.

There is no soundtrack. On phones that vibrate through the speaker, its low
vibration tone can be audible; see [Vibration](/fplinux/use/display-and-lights/#vibration).

## Exit

Press <Key>Right soft</Key> at any time. The display returns to the terminal, the
LCD returns to your brightness level, including a level you changed while the
presentation ran, the keypad light returns to its level before the start, and
the vibration stops.

After it exits, the application prints one line of figures for the run: the
number of completed cycles, the frames shown, frame rates and frame times, its
CPU use and its peak memory.
